## TL;DR

FlashStieltjes is my honors thesis at Ohio State. It is a GPU kernel for **Stieltjes attention**, a drop-in replacement for softmax that weighs keys with a power law instead of an exponential. Written in Triton in the style of FlashAttention, it runs the forward pass **3.37× faster** than a PyTorch implementation on an A100 and never builds the N×N attention matrix.

## Why touch softmax

Every Transformer turns attention scores into weights with softmax. It works great, but it is only one of many functions that take a list of scores and return positive numbers that add up to 1. Softmax does it with an exponential: every step down in score shrinks the weight by the same factor, so far-away keys end up with practically nothing.

Stieltjes attention uses a different rule:

$$
p_j = (\lambda - s_j)^{-q}
$$

Here $s_j$ is the score of key $j$, $q$ controls how sharp the attention is, and $\lambda$ (the "pole") sits just above the best score. You can think of $\lambda$ as a viewpoint: every key is weighed by how far its score is from that point, and the point slides until all weights add up to exactly 1. The weights now fall off like a power law. In this example the best keys stand out more, and the long tail of weak keys never quite drops to zero:

## The challenge

Softmax is easy to compute in one go: add up the exponentials as the keys stream by and divide at the end. That is what makes FlashAttention possible. Stieltjes has no such luck. The pole $\lambda$ depends on every score in the row, and there is no formula for it, so it has to be found by trial and error.

I use Newton's method for that. After subtracting each row's largest score, the same starting guess of 1.1 works for every row, and it converges in 2-3 steps for $q \le 4$.

## The kernel

The usual way to compute attention builds a table of all N×N scores, and that table is what eats the memory at long sequences. A flash-style kernel keeps one block of queries in the GPU's fast memory and streams the keys past it in tiles instead. Since Stieltjes cannot finish in one pass, FlashStieltjes streams them five times:

Memory now grows with the sequence length and not with its square.

To check that swapping softmax out doesn't hurt, I trained small nanoGPT models on algorithmic tasks like finding a needle, sorting, binary search, and BFS. Stieltjes attention matched softmax within 0.03 accuracy on every one of them.
